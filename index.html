About

I am an MSc student in Information and Network Engineering at KTH Royal Institute of Technology. Before KTH, I received my BSc in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Shanghai Jiao Tong University.

My current research lies at the intersection of 3D vision, multimodal learning, and reliable AI. I am particularly interested in learning meaningful representations from incomplete 3D observations and in understanding how model failures can be detected, localized, and corrected.

In the longer term, I hope to develop clinically reliable world models that integrate spatial, visual, textual, and longitudinal information into an evolving representation of patient state.

VecSetX

3D anatomical representation and completion from partial observations

VecSetX studies how generative 3D models can infer missing anatomical structures from incomplete observations across heterogeneous medical imaging datasets.

My work includes building and validating a unified 30-class anatomical representation across multiple datasets, designing leak-safe completion experiments, and analyzing whether conditional generative models genuinely use the available anatomical context.

The project is being developed into a manuscript for submission to CVPR 2027. I am a co-first author.

Pulse

Reliable 3D pulmonary-tree analysis and structural quality assessment

Pulse investigates structural failure modes in 3D airway segmentation, with an emphasis on connectivity, component structure, prediction confidence, and errors that are not well captured by aggregate metrics.

I led reproducible failure characterization on a 50-case ATM22 diagnostic cohort and analyzed the limitations of naive largest-component filtering, including cases where valid, high-confidence airway structures are removed.

The next stage explores inference-time structural evaluation and localized feedback for candidate repair and re-evaluation.
